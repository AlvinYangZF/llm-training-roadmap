Fine-tune a 70B model with 0.1 % of the parameters.

LoRA freezes the pretrained weights and adds two small matrices whose product approximates the update. Train fewer params, swap adapters per task, merge at deploy.

Pretraining made the base model expensive. Full fine-tuning makes every downstream task expensive too — same weight count, same gradient memory. LoRA observes that the update is low-rank, even when the weight matrix isn\'t.

The trick

Freeze pretrained matrix W (d × d). Instead of learning ΔW (also d × d), learn two thin matrices: B (d × r) and A (r × d), where r is small (4 – 64). The effective update is BA. At inference, fuse W + BA — back to a single matmul, zero overhead.

h = W·x + B·A·x
// W frozen · only B and A receive gradients · params: 2dr instead of d²

The cost numbers

For Llama-2-7B, a typical LoRA targets only the attention Q/V matrices with rank 8:

ApproachTrainable paramsVRAM (train)Per-task storage
Full fine-tune7.0 B~80 GB14 GB

LoRA r=64 (all linear)160 M~22 GB320 MB
QLoRA r=84.2 M~6 GB17 MB
Why it matters → A 17 MB adapter is git-able. You can ship 100 task-specific adapters next to one base model and hot-swap them. This is how almost every fine-tuned model on Hugging Face is actually distributed.

QLoRA · the cost killer

Quantize the frozen base to 4-bit (NF4 — a custom dtype optimized for normally-distributed weights). Keep LoRA matrices in bf16. Compute happens in bf16 after dequantizing chunks on-the-fly. The base stays small in memory; gradients only flow through B and A. Result: 70B fine-tuning fits on a single 48 GB card.
