Attention, rewritten as an IO problem.

Standard attention spends 80% of its time moving an N×N matrix between HBM and SRAM. FlashAttention never writes it — just tiles, streams, and keeps a running softmax. Same output, 2–4× faster, O(N) memory.

The GPU on your desk has two memories. A huge slow one (HBM) and a tiny fast one (SRAM). Standard attention treats them as if they were interchangeable — it isn't. Once you realize attention is bottlenecked on where data lives, not how many FLOPs it needs, the whole algorithm gets rewritten.

The GPU memory hierarchy

An A100 has ~40–80 GB of HBM at ~1.5 TB/s and ~20 MB of SRAM at ~19 TB/s. That 13× bandwidth gap is what FlashAttention exploits. If your algorithm touches HBM a lot, you'll saturate its bandwidth long before you run out of FLOPs.

Standard vs Flash the whole story in one picture

Standard attention materializes a full N×N matrix in HBM: write it, read it back for softmax, read it again to multiply by V. That's 3 passes over O(N²) bytes. FlashAttention never materializes it at all.

Tiling split, stream, accumulate

Break Q and K into blocks of size $B_r \times B_c$ where $B_c = M / (4d)$ — just small enough that a pair of tiles fits in SRAM with room for intermediates. For each query tile, stream through all key tiles, compute partial attention on-chip, accumulate the output. That's it.

Online softmax the clever trick that makes it work

You can't softmax one tile at a time and just sum — softmax is global. The online softmax trick fixes this by tracking a running max $m_i$ and running denominator $\ell_i$. When a new tile's max is larger, you rescale your previous partial sum by $e^{m_{\text{old}} - m_{\text{new}}}$. Mathematically identical to global softmax; numerically stable; requires a single streaming pass.

// online softmax update
$$m_{\text{new}} = \max(m_{\text{old}}, \max(x_{\text{block}})), \quad \ell_{\text{new}} = e^{m_{\text{old}} - m_{\text{new}}} \cdot \ell_{\text{old}} + \sum e^{x_{\text{block}} - m_{\text{new}}}$$
