Billion-scale ANN with 10 MB of RAM.

AiSAQ takes DiskANN further: even the PQ table stays on SSD. RAM is reduced from ~30 GB to ~10 MB. The cost: one extra disk seek per hop. The benefit: hot-swap indexes in milliseconds, run dozens of tenants on one box.

DiskANN was already memory-frugal — but in multi-tenant RAG (one index per customer, hundreds of customers), even 30 GB × 100 tenants is impossible. AiSAQ\'s answer: store all vector data on SSD, including the PQ codebook.

The hot-swap superpower

Because RAM footprint is tiny, you can have thousands of indexes mmap\'d simultaneously. Switching from tenant A to tenant B is a pointer change, not an index load. Index switch latency drops from seconds to sub-millisecond — opening up workloads like per-conversation RAG (one index per chat session) that were uneconomic before.

Cost · the extra seek

Every hop in the graph needs the PQ codes for neighbours, which now come from SSD. AiSAQ co-locates PQ codes with adjacency on disk — one read per hop returns both edges and codes. Net latency cost: ~30–50 % over DiskANN, with SSD throughput being the bottleneck.
