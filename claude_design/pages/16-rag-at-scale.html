Param efficiency

"Make the model bigger" hits a wall. RETRO\'s answer: keep the model small, push knowledge into a non-parametric index. The architectural pattern — and the operational taxonomy that grew from it — is now industry-standard.

RETRO · the 2022 paper

Borgeaud et al, DeepMind, 2022. Architecturally: every chunk of 64 tokens in the input is used as a query against a 2-trillion-token retrieval index. The top-k neighbours are encoded by a frozen BERT and cross-attended into the main Transformer via dedicated RETRO blocks. Training is end-to-end except the retriever.

Key insight. Retrieval gives access to facts seen during training as well — it\'s not just for "new" knowledge. The model learns to defer factual recall to the index and use its parameters for reasoning and composition.

Three generations · the taxonomy

Gao et al, 2024 survey, codified what production teams were already shipping:

Gen 01 · Naive RAG

Retrieve → augment → generate.

One vector index. One similarity search. Stuff top-k into the prompt. Most demos. Brittle.

  • FAISS or Pinecone
  • Single-step retrieval
  • No grading, no fusion
Gen 02 · Advanced

Pre + post-retrieval optimization.

Query rewriting (HyDE), chunk reranking (cross-encoders), context compression, hybrid scoring (BM25 + dense).

  • HyDE · rerank · RRF
  • Recursive retrieval
  • Chunk overlap tuning
Gen 03 · Modular

Composable retrieval orchestration.

Decoupled router, retriever, generator, memory, validator. Each module swappable. Self-RAG, CRAG, FLARE all live here.

  • Router → retriever → critic
  • Multi-hop, agentic
  • Tool-use blends in

What ships in 2025

  • Hybrid retrieval · BM25 + dense + cross-encoder rerank is the boring default; chapter 20
  • Late interaction · ColBERT v2 / PLAID — token-level scoring at index time
  • Long-context contests RAG · 1M-token context windows make naive RAG less compelling for some workloads — but cost still favours retrieval at scale
  • Cache-Augmented Generation · CAG preloads the corpus into the KV cache; useful for small-corpus, high-QPS apps. chapter 22
