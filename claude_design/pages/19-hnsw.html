How to find a needle in a billion-vector haystack.

HNSW — Hierarchical Navigable Small World — is the default ANN index in Faiss, Pinecone, Milvus, Qdrant, and every vector database you've heard of. It's a graph that looks like a subway map, and it's almost absurdly effective.

Malkov & Yashunin

M, ef
Two main knobs

Exhaustive nearest-neighbor search is simple, correct, and useless at scale. A billion vectors × 768 dims × 4 bytes = 3 TB of data touched per query. HNSW trades a tiny amount of accuracy for a thousandfold speedup by navigating a layered graph whose shortcuts shrink the search space exponentially.

The small-world insight why six degrees of separation matters

In a "small-world" graph, any two nodes are connected by a short path — even though most connections are local. Your brain is one. So is the internet. So is the friendship graph on Facebook.

The trick: layer the graph. Most nodes live only at the bottom layer with dense local connections. A few lucky nodes get promoted to higher layers with sparser, longer-range edges. Search starts at the top (big jumps, small layer), drops down each time it finds a "good enough" neighbor, and finishes on the dense bottom floor.

// intuition
Think of it as a multi-resolution map. Top layer is the subway — 5 stations, gets you across town. Middle is the bus network. Bottom is walking the street. You use the subway until you're close, then descend to fine-grained navigation.

The algorithm in 6 lines deceptively simple

// greedy search
fn search(q, entry):
  cur = entry
  for layer in [max_layer … 1]:
    cur = greedy_descend(q, cur, layer)   // big jumps
  return beam_search(q, cur, layer=0, ef)  // fine-grained

greedy_descend looks at a node's neighbors, jumps to whichever is closer to the query, repeats until no neighbor improves. beam_search on the bottom layer keeps the $ef$ closest candidates and explores their neighborhoods until the frontier stops shrinking. That's it.

The two knobs M and ef

M — edges per node

Controls graph density. Higher M = better recall, bigger index. Typical: 16–32. Set at build time, permanent.

ef — search beam width

Controls query quality. Higher ef = more neighbors explored = higher recall, higher latency. Set per-query. 10 → fast, 200 → careful.
// deletes are the pain point
HNSW has no native "remove a node" operation. Production systems either rebuild periodically, tombstone-and-skip, or use a deletion log. This is the biggest operational gotcha in every vector DB.
