Retrieve, then generate.

RAG is a two-step procedure: fetch the relevant evidence from a knowledge store, then hand it to the LLM as context. It's the reason modern chatbots can cite today's news without ever being trained on it.

A parametric model knows only what it saw during training. RAG flips that: freeze the model, put your knowledge in an external store, and inject the top-k relevant chunks into the prompt at query time. Your model gets current knowledge, cited answers, and updatable facts — without a single gradient step.

Chunking before any of this works

Documents must be split before indexing. A naïve split (every 512 tokens, no overlap) is almost always wrong — sentences get cut, context disappears. Modern chunking strategies:

  • Fixed-size with overlap. 512 tokens, 64-token overlap. Safe default.
  • Recursive. Split on paragraph, then sentence, then token. Preserves semantic boundaries.
  • Semantic. Embed sentences, group by cosine similarity. Expensive but quality-aware.
  • Structure-aware. Respect markdown headings, HTML sections, code blocks.
// common failure
"Our RAG doesn't work." 90% of the time, your chunks are either too small (no context for the LLM) or your split mid-sentence (ruins the embedding). Before debugging the retriever, look at 50 random chunks.

HyDE hallucinate to retrieve better

A query like "what's long covid?" is 4 tokens. A real answer passage is 200 tokens, filled with technical vocabulary your 4-token query doesn't contain. The embedding gap can be brutal.

HyDE (Hypothetical Document Embeddings, Gao et al. 2022) sidesteps this: ask the LLM to hallucinate an answer first, then embed that hallucinated answer and retrieve against it. The fake answer lands much closer to real answers in embedding space than the short query ever did.

The RAG formula generation conditioned on retrieval

// marginalization over retrieved docs
$$p(y \mid x) = \sum_{z \in \text{top-k}(p_{\eta}(\cdot \mid x))} p_{\eta}(z \mid x) \cdot p_{\theta}(y \mid x, z)$$
retriever $p_{\eta}$ scores docs; generator $p_{\theta}$ produces answers; output marginalizes over top-k.

In practice, production systems skip the marginalization: concatenate the top-k docs into the prompt and let the LLM attend over them. It's sloppy, it's fast, it works.
