Words become vectors, vectors become geometry.

An embedding maps a token, a sentence, or a whole document to a point in $\mathbb{R}^d$. The trick is: similar meanings end up close together, and arithmetic in vector space mirrors arithmetic in meaning. Every retriever, classifier, and reranker rides on this.

Computers don't understand "cat" — but they understand a 768-dimensional vector that happens to sit very close to the vector for "kitten". Embeddings are how we smuggle meaning into linear algebra.

From symbols to vectors the one-hot wasteland

The naive way to encode words is one-hot: vocabulary of 50,000 → each word is a vector with a single 1 and 49,999 zeros. This works, but it's terrible: cos("cat", "kitten") = 0, same as cos("cat", "lawnmower") = 0. Geometry tells us nothing.

A learned embedding matrix $E \in \mathbb{R}^{|V| \times d}$ replaces this with $d$-dimensional dense vectors (typically $d = 256$ to $1536$). Each row is a word's coordinates in semantic space. During training, gradients pull related words together and push unrelated ones apart.

Cosine similarity direction is meaning

Once words are vectors, "similar" means "pointing in similar directions". The standard metric is cosine similarity:

// cosine similarity — angle between two vectors
$$\text{cos}(u, v) \;=\; \frac{u \cdot v}{\|u\|\,\|v\|} \;\in\; [-1, +1]$$

+1 means perfectly aligned (synonyms, near-paraphrases). 0 means orthogonal (unrelated). −1 means opposite. Magnitude doesn't matter — only direction. This is why embedding models almost always L2-normalize at the end.

Geometry of meaning analogies are arithmetic

The famous result from word2vec: vector arithmetic captures relationships. The classic example:

// the most-quoted equation in NLP
$$\vec{\text{king}} - \vec{\text{man}} + \vec{\text{woman}} \approx \vec{\text{queen}}$$

Subtracting "man" from "king" isolates the concept of royalty without male-ness. Add "woman" and you land near "queen". The model never saw this equation — it emerged from learning to predict surrounding words.

These analogies work surprisingly well — but they're not perfect, and they encode every bias in the training corpus. Doctor − man + woman often returns "nurse". The geometry is a mirror of the data.

Token vs sentence vs document three scales

Embeddings come in different granularities, and they're built differently:

  • Token embeddings. A row in the LLM's input matrix. Lookup-based, learned during pretraining. Used inside the model.
  • Sentence / passage embeddings. A single vector per text chunk. Learned via contrastive training (positive pairs pulled together, negatives pushed apart). Used by retrieval.
  • Document embeddings. Sentence embeddings, mean-pooled or recombined. Used for clustering, dedup, dataset curation.

For retrieval, the workhorse is sentence-transformers style: take an LLM's last hidden states, mean-pool, L2-normalize. Fine-tune with InfoNCE loss on (query, positive, hard-negative) triplets.

Why this matters downstream three big use cases

  • Retrieval (Ch. 12, 13). Embed the query, embed the docs, top-k by cosine. The whole RAG stack.
  • Clustering & dedup. Group similar docs without labels. Used heavily in pretraining data prep.
  • Reranking signals. Cosine sim becomes a feature in a richer cross-encoder ranker.
