The lexical baseline that still wins.

BM25 is thirty years old, runs on a single CPU, and beats half the modern dense retrievers on out-of-domain benchmarks. Three knobs — k₁, b, and your tokenizer — and an inverted index. That\'s the whole thing.

Every "we tried embeddings, BM25 was better" tweet is real. Lexical retrieval has near-zero training data dependence, no embedding drift, and a deterministic failure mode. Knowing it cold is non-negotiable.

The formula

$$\text{BM25}(q,d) = \sum_{t \in q} \text{IDF}(t) \cdot \frac{f(t,d) \cdot (k_1+1)}{f(t,d) + k_1 \cdot (1 - b + b \cdot \frac{|d|}{\bar{d}})}$$
// f = term freq · |d| = doc length · d̄ = avg length · IDF = log((N − n + 0.5)/(n + 0.5) + 1)

The two knobs · k₁ and b

k₁ controls how fast term frequency saturates — high k₁, the 5th occurrence still matters; low k₁, after 1-2 occurrences nothing changes. b controls how much you penalize long documents — b=0, ignore length; b=1, fully normalize.

Inverted index · the data structure

BM25 doesn\'t scan documents at query time. It looks up each query term in an inverted index — a dict from term to posting list (doc id, term frequency). Sum across query terms, sort, return top-k.
