Most attention is wasted on almost no one.

In a real attention layer, ~5% of tokens absorb ~80% of the attention mass. Keep those — and a handful of "sink" tokens — and the rest of the KV cache can be thrown out. 20× compression, <3% perplexity hit.

Attention is dense in shape but sparse in practice. If you actually look at the weight matrix on a real model, almost everything is dark. The H2O paper noticed this empirical regularity, named the bright dots "heavy hitters," and built an eviction policy around them.

The H2O eviction loop sinks · heavy-hitters · drop the rest

At each generation step, score every active token by its cumulative attention. Keep the first 4 tokens (the "sinks" — empirically essential), keep the top heavy-hitters under your budget, evict everyone else.

20% is the sweet spot for OPT-6.7B and similar. Below 10%, perplexity degrades fast — you've started evicting actual heavy-hitters, not noise.

Why both sinks and heavy-hitters

Three retention strategies, same 20% budget:

recent window only

Last N tokens. Loses long-range context. Output drifts off-topic.
