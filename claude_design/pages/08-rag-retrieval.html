The retriever decides the ceiling.

An LLM can\'t answer from documents it never saw. RAG\'s top-line accuracy is bounded by recall@k of the retrieval step — and recall depends entirely on whether you matched words, meaning, or both. Lexical, dense, and hybrid each fail in different ways.

Retrieval is the half of RAG most teams underinvest in. Generation can\'t fix retrieval errors — it can only paper over them. Spend a day on your retriever and a week of "prompt engineering" disappears.

Two ways to find a passage

Lexical matches surface tokens — a query and document share the word "transformer," they connect. Dense matches meaning — "neural attention" finds "self-attention" even with zero word overlap. Each catches what the other misses. The bug pattern is asymmetric:

// failure modes
Lexical-only · misses paraphrases, synonyms, multilingual.
Dense-only · misses rare terms, codes (ICD-10, error codes), exact names.
Both · fail on multi-hop or implicit reasoning queries (chapter 09).

Recall@k is the budget

$$\text{end-to-end accuracy} \le \text{Recall@k} \times \text{generator accuracy given gold passage}$$
// generator can\'t fix what retriever didn\'t fetch.

If recall@5 is 70%, your RAG accuracy ceiling is 70% — even with a perfect generator. Push retrieval before tuning prompts. This is also why "give the LLM the whole document" is sometimes the right play: it makes recall@k = 1 by force, at the cost of context length.
