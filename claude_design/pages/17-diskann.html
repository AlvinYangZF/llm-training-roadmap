A billion vectors. One server. 64 GB of RAM.

DiskANN puts the whole graph on SSD, keeps a tiny index in memory, and does ~10ms recall@95. The Vamana graph is the trick — short edges for accuracy, long edges for navigation, on disk.

HNSW is fast — but you need the whole graph in RAM. At 1B vectors × 768 dim × 4 bytes, that\'s 3 TB. DiskANN solves this with a single-layer graph designed for SSD-friendly traversal, plus a quantized in-memory cache.

Vamana · the graph

HNSW uses multiple layers; DiskANN uses one. The Vamana graph is built greedily with a parameter α ≥ 1: when adding edges, prune candidates that can be reached via existing edges of length ≤ α × direct distance. α = 1 gives the tightest graph (fewest edges); α > 1 retains some long-range edges that help navigation.

Search · greedy with PQ

At query time: start at a fixed entry node, fetch its neighbours from disk, score using product-quantized approximations of the vectors in RAM, pick the closest unvisited, repeat. Re-rank the final candidates with full-precision vectors loaded from SSD. Average disk reads per query: ~50–200.

Why one layer works

HNSW\'s upper layers exist to route — to skip across the graph quickly. Vamana achieves this with a single layer because α > 1 keeps long edges in the same graph. The result: less metadata, fewer disk seeks, simpler eviction.
